# "WISH YOU WERE HER"
### A dark-comedy short — shooting script
**Runtime target:** 60–90 seconds (works as a Short/Reel or a scene inside a longer video)
**Music cue:** "The End" — The Doors ("This is the end, beautiful friend…")
> ⚠️ For YouTube monetization use a royalty-free soundalike (slow, doomy, cinematic) — the actual Doors track will get claimed. The cue is marked **[SONG]** below.

---

## CHARACTERS
- **DANIEL (38)** — married, out alone on a work trip / evening walk. Thinks he's funny. He is about to find out he is not.
- **THE COUPLE** — mid-20s, stupidly in love, golden-hour lighting, the works. No lines.
- **PRIYA (V.O. via phone)** — Daniel's wife. Never seen. Only a chat bubble. That's what makes it scary.

---

## SCENE 1 — EXT. SEASIDE PROMENADE / PARK — GOLDEN HOUR

Wide shot. DANIEL walks alone, coffee in hand, jacket over shoulder. Relaxed. A man at peace. (Enjoy it, Daniel.)

He stops. Something catches his eye.

**DANIEL'S POV:** A COUPLE on a bench. She's laughing at something he said. He tucks her hair behind her ear. It's disgustingly romantic. A movie poster of a moment.

**CLOSE ON DANIEL:** A slow grin spreads. Not malice — mischief. He's already composing the joke in his head.

> **DANIEL** *(muttering, to himself)*
> Oh, that's good. That's *good.*

He raises his phone. **CLICK.** (Insert: shutter animation on screen.)

---

## SCENE 2 — INSERT: PHONE SCREEN — CONTINUOUS

**FULL-SCREEN PHONE UI.** Chat with **"Priya ❤️"**.

The photo of the couple attaches. Cursor blinks. He types:

> **wish you were her** ✈️ **SEND.**

**ONE checkmark. TWO checkmarks.**

**BACK ON DANIEL:** smug, sips coffee, starts walking. Then —

**HE STOPS MID-STRIDE.** Frame freezes on his face for half a beat.

> **DANIEL** *(whisper)*
> …her?

**INSERT — PHONE:** He re-reads it. **"wish you were her"** — not *here*. **HER.**

---

## SCENE 3 — THE FUMBLE — CONTINUOUS

Handheld now. Panic camera. Daniel's thumbs move like he's defusing a bomb.

Long-press the message → menu pops up:

> **Delete for Everyone**
> **Delete for Me**

His thumb — trembling, coffee-jittery — taps.

**SMASH CUT — INSERT, EXTREME CLOSE-UP:**

> ✓ *Message deleted **for you**.*

Beat. The chat on HIS phone is clean. He exhales, laughs, relieved—

**INSERT — PHONE:** Under the message *on her side*, the two checkmarks turn **BLUE**.

> **Priya ❤️ is typing…**

---

## SCENE 4 — THE NEEDLE DROP 🎯

**EXTREME CLOSE-UP:** Daniel's eyes. The exact frame the blue ticks register.

### **[SONG] — "THIS IS THE END…" begins EXACTLY here.**

Everything goes SLOW MOTION:

- The coffee cup slips from his hand — falls — hits the pavement — explodes in slo-mo.
- The couple on the bench, background, out of focus, still kissing. Mocking him.
- Daniel turns to camera. Dead eyes. The 1000-yard stare.

**INSERT — PHONE, final shot of the thread:**

> **Priya ❤️:** *typing…*
> **Priya ❤️:** *typing stops.*
> **Priya ❤️:** **"📞 Missed call"**
> **Priya ❤️:** **"📞 Missed call"**
> **Priya ❤️:** **"Come home. Now."**

---

## SCENE 5 — OUTRO — SAME LOCATION, WIDE

Song continues, swelling. Wide static shot: golden hour has died into blue dusk. Daniel sits alone on the OTHER bench, mirroring the couple's pose — but alone, staring at the sea.

The couple walks past behind him, hand in hand, happy.

**TEXT ON SCREEN:**

> **"Delete for Everyone. ALWAYS Delete for Everyone."**

**HARD CUT TO BLACK.** Song cuts dead mid-note.

Beat of silence. Then, in the dark:

> **PHONE BUZZ.** *(audio only)*

**END.**

# 🎬 SCENE ANALYSIS — WHERE THE SONG HITS BEST

## The verdict: **Scene 4, frame-exact on the blue ticks.** Not before, not after.

There are four candidate spots for the needle drop. Here's why three of them lose:

### ❌ Option A — When he sends the message (Scene 2)
Too early. The audience hasn't caught the typo yet — half of them will read "wish you were her" as "here" the first time, exactly like Daniel did. Dropping the song here spends your biggest weapon before the joke is armed. You'd be scoring a scene the audience doesn't know is a disaster yet.

### ❌ Option B — When he notices the typo ("…her?")
Tempting, but wrong. This is the *setup* of the second joke, not the punchline. At this moment there's still hope — he can delete it. Comedy needs that hope alive and silent. Music here kills the tension instead of building it. Keep Scene 2–3 with **no music at all**: just ambient sound, seagulls, his breathing. Silence is what makes the drop land later.

### ✅ Option C — THE WINNER: the moment the ticks turn blue after "Deleted for you" (Scene 4)
This is the point of **no return** — the precise frame where hope dies:
1. **Irony is complete.** He did everything right in his head and still lost. "Deleted for me" means *he* can't even see the evidence, but *she* can. The punishment fits the crime perfectly.
2. **The lyric is literal.** "This is the end, beautiful friend" over a man watching his marriage combust via read receipts — the song stops being background and becomes the narrator.
3. **It converts comedy → mock-tragedy.** The slow-mo coffee drop + Doors-style doom turns a 5-second phone gag into an Apocalypse-Now funeral. That tonal whiplash IS the joke. This is the frame people clip and share.
4. **Structurally, it's the golden-ratio point** — roughly 60–65% into the runtime, exactly where a climax belongs in a sub-90-second format.

### ❌ Option D — The outro bench shot (Scene 5)
Good scene, wrong job. If the song only starts here, it's an epilogue score — sad, but not funny. Instead, let the song **carry over** from Scene 4 into Scene 5 and then cut it dead at the hard cut to black. The mid-note cut + single phone buzz in darkness is a stronger ending than any lyric.

## Sound design map
| Section | Audio |
|---|---|
| Scene 1–2 | Ambient only (waves, birds, footsteps). NO music. |
| Scene 3 (the fumble) | Ambient + heartbeat thump, subtle riser |
| **Scene 4 (blue ticks)** | **RISER CUTS → SONG SLAMS IN. Slow-mo. Everything else muted.** |
| Scene 5 | Song swells, then HARD CUT to silence → one phone buzz |

## One production note
The entire skit lives or dies on **two inserts**: the "Delete for Me" tap and the "✓ Message deleted for you" confirmation. Shoot the phone screen as a real screen recording, not a re-creation — audiences instantly smell fake UI, and the joke depends on them recognizing the exact menu they've fumbled themselves.
