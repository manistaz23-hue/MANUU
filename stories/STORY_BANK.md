## ✅ 001 — The Man Who Put His Head in a Particle Accelerator
**Hook:** In 1978, a Soviet scientist put his head inside a running particle accelerator — and survived.
**Payoff:** The paralyzed half of his face never aged again.
**Facts:** Anatoli Bugorski, June 3 1978, U-70 synchrotron, Protvino USSR. 76 GeV proton
beam entered the back of his skull, exited near his nose. Saw a flash "brighter than a
thousand suns", felt no pain. Local dose 200,000–300,000 roentgens — hundreds of times
lethal, but only along a narrow channel. Lost hearing in left ear, left face paralyzed,
survived, finished his PhD, kept working as a physicist.
**Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoli_Bugorski · qz.com/964065 · allthatsinteresting.com/anatoli-bugorski

## ✅ 011 — The Burglar Who Picked the Wrong Grandma (scripted as scripts/002)
**Hook:** In 2019, a man broke into the home of an 82-year-old grandmother. Big mistake.
**Payoff:** He left in an ambulance — the very thing he'd knocked on her door asking for. She posed for selfies with police.
**Facts:** Willie Murphy, Rochester NY, Nov 21 2019, ~11pm. Claimed sickness, begged for an
ambulance; she called police, refused entry; he broke through the door. She was a WNPF
champion powerlifter (2014 lifter of the year), deadlifting 225 lbs into her 80s. In the
dark she broke a table over him, poured baby shampoo in his eyes, hit him with a broom.
Quote: "I'm alone and I'm old, but guess what? I'm TOUGH."
**Sources:** washingtonpost.com/sports/2019/11/25 · abcnews.com id 67289683 · spectrumlocalnews.com 2019/11/26

## 🟡 002 — The Dancing Plague of 1518
**Hook:** In 1518, a woman started dancing in the street — and couldn't stop. Within a month, four hundred people had joined her.
**Payoff:** The city's cure was to hire musicians and make them dance MORE. People danced until they collapsed; some reportedly died.
**Verify:** Strasbourg, July 1518, Frau Troffea; historian John Waller's research.

## 🟡 003 — Mike the Headless Chicken
**Hook:** In 1945, a farmer chopped off a chicken's head — and the chicken lived for another 18 months.
**Payoff:** "Mike" went on tour, was insured for $10,000, and Fruita, Colorado still holds a festival for him every year.
**Verify:** Lloyd Olsen, Fruita CO, Sept 1945; axe missed the brain stem and jugular.

## ⬜ 004 — The Lake That Exploded
**Hook:** One night in 1986, a lake in Cameroon silently released a cloud that killed 1,700 people in their sleep.
**Payoff:** Engineers now keep the lake "burped" with pipes so it can never do it again.
**Verify:** Lake Nyos, Aug 21 1986, limnic eruption, CO2 cloud; degassing pipes since 2001.

## ⬜ 005 — The Green Children of Woolpit
**Hook:** In the 1100s, two children with green skin walked out of a pit in an English village, speaking a language nobody knew.
**Payoff:** The girl's skin faded to normal once she changed diet — but she never fully explained where they came from.
**Verify:** Medieval chronicles of Ralph of Coggeshall and William of Newburgh; present as legend/chronicle, not settled fact.

## ⬜ 006 — The Boy Who Built a Nuclear Reactor in His Shed
**Hook:** In 1994, a 17-year-old Boy Scout tried to build a nuclear breeder reactor in his mom's backyard shed.
**Payoff:** The EPA declared the shed a Superfund cleanup site. He got his radioactive material from smoke detectors and camping lanterns.
**Verify:** David Hahn, Commerce Township, Michigan; Harper's 1998 article, "The Radioactive Boy Scout".

## ⬜ 007 — The Man Who Was Struck by Lightning Seven Times
**Hook:** Lightning found the same park ranger seven separate times — and he survived every single strike.
**Payoff:** Roy Sullivan holds the Guinness record; he began carrying water in his car because his hair kept catching fire.
**Verify:** Roy Sullivan, Shenandoah National Park, strikes 1942–1977, Guinness World Records.

## ⬜ 008 — The Unsinkable Violet Jessop
**Hook:** One nurse was aboard the Titanic when it sank, the Britannic when it sank, and the Olympic when it crashed.
**Payoff:** All three were sister ships. She kept working on ocean liners for another 30 years.
**Verify:** Violet Jessop; Olympic collision 1911, Titanic 1912, Britannic 1916.

## ⬜ 009 — The Molasses Flood
**Hook:** In 1919, a 25-foot wave moving at 35 miles per hour destroyed a Boston neighborhood. The wave was made of molasses.
**Payoff:** Locals claimed the streets smelled of molasses on hot days for decades afterward.
**Verify:** Great Molasses Flood, Jan 15 1919, North End Boston, 21 dead; tank of 2.3M gallons.

## ⬜ 010 — The Phantom Barber of Pascagoula
**Hook:** In 1942, someone kept breaking into homes at night in a Mississippi town — not to steal, but to cut people's hair while they slept.
**Payoff:** A man was convicted, then widely believed innocent; the real Phantom Barber was never conclusively identified.
**Verify:** Pascagoula, Mississippi, June 1942; wartime newspaper coverage — present unresolved parts as unresolved.
