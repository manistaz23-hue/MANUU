## HOW TO READ THIS SCRIPT

- **[YOU — ON CAMERA]** = you talking to your camera (podcast setup / desk shot)
- **[INSERT CLIP]** = cut to the actual news footage. Each clip block tells you the source,
  what portion to use, and the on-screen credit to display.
- **[VO OVER CLIP]** = your voice continues while the clip plays muted or ducked (-20dB)
- **[LOWER THIRD]** = text overlay to add in edit

**Fair-use ground rules (protects the video from claims/strikes):**
1. Keep every news clip **under 20 seconds** per cut.
2. **Never let a clip play silently on its own** — either talk over it, or pause it and react.
   Your commentary is what makes it transformative.
3. Always show the **on-screen credit** for the outlet.
4. Don't use another creator's full segment structure — clips are evidence inside YOUR argument.

## SEGMENT 4 — THE BIGGER QUESTION (5:45 – 6:45)

**[YOU — ON CAMERA — this is your opinion/analysis section, slow down]**

> I want to zoom out, because this is bigger than Memphis.
>
> There's a reason America has, for most of its history, kept a bright line
> between the military and domestic policing. Soldiers and police have
> different training, different missions, and different rules.
> A cop is trained to de-escalate. A soldier is trained to win a fight.
>
> Now — to be fair to the other side of this argument — the man was reportedly
> armed, it was 4 a.m., and there were shots-fired calls. Police officers in the
> exact same situation might have made the exact same decision.
> The TBI investigation will tell us more.
>
> But that's precisely the point: we don't actually know which rules applied
> on that street on Saturday morning. And in a country of laws,
> "we don't know whose rules applied" is the scariest sentence there is.
