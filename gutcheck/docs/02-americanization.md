# 02 · Americanization

## The honest read on the brief

The reference video is not foreign content that needs localizing. It is American — it is
Black American comedic voice, AAVE-inflected, in the "coworkers arguing" tradition that
runs from Martin through Boondocks through the current wave of TikTok organ-comedy. It
is already appealing to Americans, and it did the numbers to prove it.

So "make it appeal to Americans" is being read here as three concrete jobs:

1. **Topic engine.** Point the format at the food arguments Americans are already having
   — Chipotle portions, 2am Taco Bell, seed oils, protein powder, Thanksgiving, gas
   station taquitos. Every episode's food is a thing a US viewer has an opinion about
   before the video starts. That opinion is free engagement.
2. **Platform-legal voice.** The reference drops six f-bombs and a slur-adjacent
   "bitch" in 71 seconds. That is a limited-ads flag on YouTube and a reach cap on TikTok.
   We keep the cadence and lose the words. Swap table below.
3. **Repeatability.** The reference is one video. This repo is a channel: one locked cast,
   one locked look, one script formula, twelve written episodes and a bank of twenty more.

## Voice register — keep vs. cut

**Keep, because this IS the register:**

- Second person and direct address between characters ("Man, I can't do that.")
- Interrupting, overlapping, repeating a phrase for emphasis ("uh-uh, uh-uh, uh-uh")
- Escalation by volume, not by vocabulary
- The rhetorical question used as an insult ("You worried about the wrong thing.")
- Naming things wrong on purpose (calling the lettuce "Romaine")
- The flat, defeated closer after 60 seconds of panic

**Cut:**

- Hard profanity (see swap table)
- Anything that reads as a health claim ("greens give you diarrhea" stated as fact)
- Body-horror specificity in the payoff — keep the doom, lose the diagnosis

## Profanity swap table

The rhythm matters more than the word. Every replacement below is the same syllable
count and same stress pattern as what it replaces, so the comic timing survives.

| Reference | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "we ain't supposed to be eating no f---ing lettuce" | "we are **not** doing lettuce today" |
| "get the f--- out of here" | "get **out**. Out. Out." |
| "this whole b---- going down" | "this whole **operation** goes down" |
| "shut the hell up" | "shut it." / "nope." |
| "oh hell" | "oh, **come on**" |
| "girl" (as address) | keep — it's register, not profanity |
| "she ate the f--- out that salad" | "she **demolished** that salad" |

House rule: **PG-13 max, and the strongest word in an episode is "hell" or "damn",
used once.** If a line only works with a hard word, the line is not written well enough
yet. Rewrite it.

## The American topic engine

Four content veins, each with permanent restock:

**Vein 1 — Fast food and chains.** Chipotle, Taco Bell at 2am, Costco food court,
Waffle House, Chick-fil-A sauce, drive-thru sweet tea. Highest volume, highest comments,
lowest effort. Every American has a take.

**Vein 2 — Wellness and gym culture.** Protein shakes, creatine, pre-workout, greens
powder, seed oil discourse, raw milk, electrolyte packets, "I'm just cutting."
Enormous US audience and an argumentative one.

**Vein 3 — Holiday and event food.** Thanksgiving plate, Super Bowl dip, Fourth of July
grill, Halloween candy, state fair fried everything, office holiday party. Seasonal spikes
you can schedule a year in advance.

**Vein 4 — Sad American realism.** Girl dinner, coffee on an empty stomach, gas station
taquito, hangover Gatorade, eating over the sink at 11pm, the sad desk salad. This is
the vein that gets saved and shared, because it is about the viewer, not the food.

## Brand naming — where it is allowed

| Surface | Brands allowed? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Video title | **Yes** | Search volume. "Eating Chipotle right now:" is the whole SEO play. |
| On-screen top caption | **Yes** | Same. It's commentary, not endorsement. |
| Spoken dialogue | Sparingly | One brand mention max; the joke should survive without it. |
| Image/video generation prompts | **Never** | The generation stack rejects brand and studio names. Describe the object: "a foil-wrapped burrito the size of a forearm", not the chain's name. |
| Sponsored episodes | Different rules | Disclose per FTC. Not covered by this repo. |

## Compliance guardrails

These are the things that actually get this format taken down or throttled:

- **No medical claims.** An organ can be *upset*. It cannot *diagnose*. "Greens are
  giving people diarrhea" is a misinformation flag; "we are not doing lettuce today" is a
  joke. Keep every health statement inside a character's obviously-unreliable opinion.
- **No eating-disorder adjacency.** Never frame a food as forbidden, never do calorie
  math, never let an organ praise not eating. Weight-loss-drug episodes get written as
  "the appetite hotline stopped calling", not as endorsement or warning.
- **No real medical brand names.** Especially GLP-1 drugs. Use "the shot" or "the pen."
- **No gore in the payoff.** The reference's last line is an anatomical injury. Ours land
  on inconvenience, not injury: "See y'all in twenty minutes" beats anything graphic.
- **Age rating.** Written to sit comfortably at TikTok's general audience and YouTube's
  advertiser-friendly bar. This is a monetization decision, not a taste one.
