# "THE COMMENT SECTION" — full spoken-word monologue (~3.5 min)

One continuous take. Presenter solo at the mic, calm and deliberate (~130 wpm,
0.95x). Comment cards from comment_cards/out/ dangle into frame on strings, one
group per movement, swaying gently; yanked up between movements. On "that's the
pinned comment," card_06 drops down alone and hangs through the final movement.

---

Somebody asked the internet a simple question: ladies... what's the most
unhinged thing that ever kept you alive? Don't say pepper spray. Say the thing
you've never told anyone. And a hundred thousand women answered. This is what
the comment section knows.

It knows you never scream "help." Nobody runs toward someone else's problem.
You scream FIRE — because everybody runs toward a spectacle. It knows you
scream "MOM" — because every mother within a mile will turn her head. It knows
you never yell "leave me alone" — strangers hear a lovers' quarrel and keep
walking. You yell "I DON'T KNOW YOU"... and a sidewalk full of strangers
becomes a room full of witnesses.

It knows that if he grabs you, you don't fight upward. You drop. Go limp. Dead
weight takes both of his hands... and your mouth still works.

The comment section keeps receipts. A bat in the car — with a glove and a
ball, because a bat alone is premeditated, but the whole set is just practice.
A decoy wallet with five fake dollars. Tip money riding home in a to-go box,
because nobody robs leftovers. A tiny can of pink spray paint — good luck
explaining to the police why you're glowing. A sweetheart of a dog trained to
growl on command, because everyone assumes she's a police dog... and she lets
them.

And when props aren't enough, it performs. It walks into an empty apartment
yelling "HONEY, I'M HOME" so the whole street believes in a man who doesn't
exist. It dates a boyfriend in the FBI who was never born. It acts unwell on
the late train — because the craziest person in the room is the one nobody
touches. One woman faked a full religious meltdown until the driver begged her
to get out of his car. Another looked down the barrel of a gun and screamed "I
WISH YOU WOULD — DO ME A FAVOR." He ran. She's the one who told us.

Some of it is quieter. A secret fund he'll never find. A fake name for
strangers — you don't owe the truth to anyone you don't know. A door that
never has to open — knock all you want; she doesn't live in customer service.
Shoes she can run in, every time she's alone. And from a 9-1-1 operator, one
for free: if you don't know where you are — mile markers, exit signs, store
names. That's how they find you. And if a stranger is creeping too close, walk
up to the nearest auntie and say "MOM — this guy is bothering me."
Congratulations. You've been adopted.

Nobody teaches this in school. You think it's just women being dramatic. You
think it's just another comment section. But every comment is a lesson
somebody paid for — posted by a woman who lived long enough to type it.

So here's what it all comes down to. Loud works. Weird works. Disgusting
works. Spit. Slobber. Bark. Sing off-key about nothing. Be impossible to
predict and impossible to want. You can wash every bit of it off later...
alive. That's not my line. That's the pinned comment.

Trust your gut — it's the one alarm that never needs charging. And remember
what somebody's momma said, in the smartest sentence on the internet: you can
always apologize for overreacting. You can't apologize if you're dead. Make. A
damn. Scene.

Because eventually... the scene you make is the life you keep.

[optional CTA] The comment section is open. Add your lesson. The wildest one
opens part two.

---

Delivery notes: beat before "FIRE"; even liturgical rhythm on the "It knows"
lines; drop volume on "Nobody teaches this in school"; one word per breath on
"Spit. Slobber. Bark."; longest pause after "alive"; slow to a near stop on
the final line and hold eye contact two seconds before cutting.
